title: Phone remote
description: Follow your Arakun sessions from a phone, send prompts and answer the agent, over your local network, Tailscale or an encrypted relay you sign in to.
---

Arakun serves a web page that lets a phone follow sessions, read them live, send prompts, stop a turn and answer the agent.

Turn on **Phone** under Arakun › Extensions, or Settings › General › Sidebar. It is already on if you chose to pair a phone in the first-run setup. Then pick how the phone reaches this computer in the Phone tab.

## How the phone reaches your computer

- **Over the local network.** This is the default. You pair the phone with a code.
- **Over Tailscale.** You pair the phone with a code.
- **Through an end-to-end encrypted relay.** You sign in to your Arakun account on the computer and on the phone, with GitHub or with a code sent by email. There is no code to pair.

On the local network and over Tailscale, the page comes from the computer itself. It only opens while the computer is on and awake. The relay works from anywhere and says when the computer is offline; the Phone tab then offers **Use Relay**.

Every way is end-to-end encrypted between the phone and the computer. The computer signs each handshake with its own key, and the phone checks it is the key it was paired with. The pairing code, the device token, the sessions and the notification settings all go inside that channel. Plain HTTP only carries the page itself, so someone on the same Wi-Fi who listens reads nothing. Someone who can change the traffic could still change the page: against that, use Tailscale, where the page comes over HTTPS, or the relay.

## Reach it through the relay

The relay lets a device in only when it is signed in to the same account as the computer.

<Steps>
<Step title="Sign in on the computer">

With Relay chosen, the Phone tab says "Sign in to reach this computer from anywhere". Press **Sign in**. It is the same sign-in as the User row of the sidebar.

The dialog offers two ways:

- **Continue with GitHub** shows a code to approve on GitHub.
- **Email me a code** sends a six-digit code to the address you type. Type it in the dialog. It works once, for 10 minutes.

Either way makes an account if you have none. An account made by email is not the same as one made with GitHub, so sign in the same way on every device, or link both to one account in Settings › Account.

Settings › Account lists how the account signs in: GitHub, an email address, or both. **Add GitHub** runs the same GitHub sign-in, and **Add address** mails a code to the address. A GitHub account or address that already signs in to another Arakun account is refused, so two accounts are never joined. **Remove** takes a method off the account, after you confirm, as long as another is left. Each change is mailed to the account's addresses.

</Step>
<Step title="Open the page on the phone">

The Phone tab shows the address to open on the phone, `https://app.arakun.dev`, with its QR code.

</Step>
<Step title="Sign in on the phone and pick the computer">

Sign in as the same account, with GitHub or with a code emailed to you, then pick the computer. With only one computer, the page opens it straight away.

</Step>
</Steps>

The first time a device connects, a notice names it and says it connected for the first time.

Signed in, the Phone tab lists:

- **Signed-in devices:** every device signed in to the account, with its kind and when it was last used. **Sign out** signs one out.
- **Your computers:** the account's computers that run the app, online or when last seen. **Remove** takes one off the account, after a question that names it. A computer still signed in comes back the next time it connects.

On the phone, each computer in the list has **Remove**, and the page's Settings tab lists the account's signed-in devices with **Sign out**. With several computers, push notifications on the page come only from the computer you opened last.

<Warning>
Anyone who signs in to your account, or holds one of its sessions, can reach every computer of the account. Sign out the devices you do not know.
</Warning>

<Note>
A phone paired through the relay with a link before v0.19.0 must sign in again to the same account. The Phone tab says so under **Sign in**. To use no account, pick Tailscale or Same Wi-Fi instead. Pairing over the local network or Tailscale is unchanged.
</Note>

## Pair a phone on the network

Over the local network or Tailscale, press **Pair a phone**. Scan the code with the phone's camera. Or open the page on the phone and type the code, or send it the link.

The code or link works once. Each phone gets a key of its own, which you can revoke under **Paired phones**. That list shows only the phones paired with a code.

The hosted relay's free plan allows 10 computers per account. Each computer takes up to 5 phones at once and 100,000 frames a day (UTC).

## Phone apps

The iOS and Android apps reach your computers the same way, only by signing in to your account. Press **Sign in with GitHub**, or type your address and press **Email me a code**, then type the six-digit code from the mail. They have no link or code for the relay.

The apps are in beta. To ask for an invite, in the Phone tab, signed in:

<Steps>
<Step title="Type the email for the invite">

Under **Phone app beta**, type your Apple ID for iOS or your Google account for Android. It starts as your GitHub email.

</Step>
<Step title="Join the beta">

Press **Join the iOS beta** or **Join the Android beta**. Testers are added by hand, so the row says you signed up until the TestFlight or Google Play invite is on its way, then that you are in. **Change email** fixes the address while you wait.

</Step>
</Steps>

The apps tell you when a turn finishes, when the agent needs a permission or an answer, and when something goes wrong. The notice says what happened: the agent's last message, the command waiting for permission, or the question. Pick which of the three you get: in Settings in the iOS app's inbox menu, under Account in the Android app's inbox menu. The Android app gets notices from every computer it has opened while they are on.

In the iOS and Android apps, the count of changed files on top of a session opens the files it changed, turn by turn. Tap a file for its diff, read-only, as on the page.

The iOS and Android apps show the same [private card](/start-a-session#when-the-agent-needs-a-secret) when an agent asks for a secret.

## When notices come

A computer, or a server, sends a phone a notice only when you are away from it: by default, after 2 minutes without using it. A permission request or a question waits until you leave, and goes only if the agent still waits. A turn shorter than 30 seconds sends no notice.

Change both under **Only when I'm away for** and **Turns shorter than this stay quiet**:

- on the page, in its Settings tab,
- in the iOS app, in Settings in the inbox menu,
- in the Android app, in Account in the inbox menu.

The bell in a session mutes that session's notices on the phone.

## Run your own relay

The relay defaults to the hosted one at `app.arakun.dev`. The relay can't hand your computer's address to anyone else: it comes from a key only your computer has.

You can run your own: it is a Cloudflare Worker in the `cloud/` folder of the repository. Point `relay_base` in `phone.json` at it, and set `ARAKUN_TEAMS_URL` to the same server. Arakun sends your session only to the server that issued it, so when the two differ the relay stays signed out and the Phone tab names both addresses.

## On a tablet or a computer

On a wider screen, the page keeps the open screen in a pane and puts a column beside it:

- **Tablet (768 px or more):** a rail with the tabs, **+**, a tile per live session and the computer.
- **Desktop browser (1,200 px or more):** a sidebar that lists your sessions by state.

The page is dark, and light when the device is set to a light appearance.

## Send prompts

While the agent works, a prompt you send waits for the turn to end, listed over the box. **Take back** puts one back in the box. With something written, **Stop** becomes **Stop and send**. **Stop** pauses what waits, and **Resume** lets it go. The iOS and Android apps do the same.

You can attach photos when you start a new session, as with any prompt. A computer on an older version starts the session without them. In the iOS app, the photo button in the composer and the new-session form opens the photo picker or the camera.

When an agent asks for a login, token or password, the page shows the same [private card](/start-a-session#when-the-agent-needs-a-secret) as the app. What you type goes only over the relay, Tailscale or https. Over plain http, the card offers only **Decline**.

## Shortcuts

The page keeps shortcuts, phrases you send often, as a row of buttons over the box.

- A shortcut that sends right away shows while the agent waits for you. The others show while you write, and fill the box.
- `{number}` in a shortcut is a blank you type each time, on the number keyboard.
- Hold a message you sent to keep it as a shortcut, or tap **+** in the row.

Shortcuts stay in that phone's browser.

## Subagents

On the page, a subagent the agent started shows as a card with its name, task, state, run time and latest step. Tap it to read the subagent's transcript, read-only. Back returns to the session. A line over the box says how many subagents run on in the background.

## Pictures and formatting

The iOS and Android apps show the photos in your prompts and the images in the agent's replies. Tap one to open it full screen. Code blocks, lists, headings, quotes and tables in a reply are formatted.

## Charts and diagrams

Charts and diagrams in a reply (` ```chart ` and ` ```mermaid ` blocks) are drawn on the phone as they are in the app. **Code** shows the block's text, and **Open** shows the drawing full screen.

## Servers

A phone connected to the app also sees and works in the sessions of your [servers](/server).

<Tip>
In redacted mode, the Phone tab hides the pairing code, its addresses and the names of paired phones. **Copy link** still copies the real link.
</Tip>
