## Open Customize

The Customize panel opens at the right of the window, where the workspace panel is, so the work stays in view while you change it. Open it with any of these:

- `Customize` in the palette,
- the View menu, on macOS,
- Settings › Appearance › Open Customize.

It opens without a project too.

## Colors

- **Mode** draws the color scheme on black, or on white with dark text.
- **Color schemes:** Arakun Black (the default), Calma, Arakun Night, Graphite, Midnight, Forest and Rosé. Duplicate one to change its tint, background and accent. Arakun warns when text or the accent gets hard to read.
- **Grays** sets how bright secondary text and borders are.
- **Diff colors** sets how added and removed lines look, with a few lines of a diff to see the choice.
- **Contrast** follows the system's high contrast setting, or is Standard or High. High raises secondary text to 7:1 and borders to 3:1, and turns Glass off. With Windows high contrast on, Arakun draws in the theme's own colors: surfaces take its window color, text its text color and the accent its link color. Your palette and mode come back with Standard, or when high contrast goes off.

**Reset colors** goes back to Arakun Black, dark, and keeps your own color schemes.

## Shape

| Setting | Range | Presets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corners | 0 to 200% | Sharp, Default, Round |
| Spacing | 60 to 160% | Compact, Default, Roomy |
| Window gap | 0 to 20 px | None, Default, Wide |

Text size and Glass are here too. **Reset shape** goes back to the defaults.

## Sidebar

Start from a preset: Needs you first, Switcher, Focused, Developer, Notion, Icons, Everything or View strip. It lays out the pieces and keeps the style you chose. Style (Card, Flush, Floating), Rows, Width, Side, what <kbd>Ctrl+B</kbd> does, Counters and Shortcuts change the whole sidebar.

**Needs you first**, the default, puts Search and the Inbox on top, then Needs you (the sessions of every project that wait on you or failed), then every project with its sessions, their finished ones folded into one "done" line. Below them it names three views (Runs, Branches and GitHub), then **More views** for the rest. It puts plan usage on one line above Settings: 5h, a bar and the percentage. The **Switcher** is the same with only the open project and its environments.

- Add a view from the chips to give it a row. Remove its row and it moves to More views.
- Select Usage to switch between the card and the line.
- A sidebar saved on the Switcher, the default before, moves to Needs you first once and keeps its style. A layout you arranged yourself stays as it is.

**View strip** puts a 40 px strip of tiles at the window's edge: Sessions, Inbox, Team and the views, More views, then plan usage, Settings and the account at its foot. Beside it, a panel holds only the sessions, the full height, with New session at its foot. Point at a tile to see its name and a live line under it, such as how many sessions need you. <kbd>Ctrl+B</kbd>, or the Sessions tile, hides the panel and keeps the strip. Any segment can be such a strip: set it to Strip instead of Fixed or Scroll.

When <kbd>Ctrl+B</kbd> folds the sidebar to icons, it shows one tile per project. A dot gives the project's worst state (amber needs you, red failed, blue working), and a marker shows the open one. Point at a tile to see that project's sessions and New session beside it. A click on a session opens it, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> or moving the pointer away closes the list. <kbd>Alt+1</kbd> to <kbd>Alt+9</kbd> open that list for the first nine projects from the keyboard: <kbd>Up</kbd> and <kbd>Down</kbd> move through it, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens a session or starts one. Search, the Inbox, Add project, More views, Settings and the account stay. The other views wait in More views.

Every preset holds the User row, a button where you sign in to your Arakun account. A layout saved without it gets it once, next to Help.

While this tab is open, you arrange the sidebar:

- A click on a piece or a title selects it. The tab then shows its options.
- Chips add a group, a link, a command, a title, a separator, a spacer or a view the sidebar doesn't hold yet.
- Drag pieces and segment titles into another order on the sidebar itself.
- The panel's header has undo and redo.

**Done** closes the panel. **Customize sidebar…** in the sidebar's right-click menu opens this tab. **Reset sidebar** goes back to the default layout and width.

<Tip>
A segment with a title folds with a click on it. Folded, it says how many pieces it holds, with a dot when one of them wants you. Right-click a title for Ungroup.
</Tip>

## Share a look

**Export…** at the foot of the panel copies the colors, the shape or the sidebar, the ones you tick, as one line that starts with `arakun:look/v1:`.

**Import** reads a look line and asks which parts to use. The sidebar is off until you tick it, since it replaces the whole layout. A sidebar layout comes without its commands: add those with Command…, where you can read what they run.

## Reduce motion

Settings › Appearance › Reduce motion holds the mascot, spinners and the island still, and shows menus and dialogs without sliding them in. It also shows or hides the sidebar and the workspace panel at once instead of sliding them.

**System**, the default, follows the system's setting: Reduce motion on macOS, Animations on GNOME, Animation effects on Windows. **On** and **Off** ignore the system.

## Use a screen reader

A screen reader can read a session's chat and its terminal.

- Each message in the chat is its own item, named for who wrote it: "You" or the agent.
- A tool call reads as what it did, such as "Edited src/main.rs, 3 lines added, 1 removed", and says when it failed.
- The terminal reads one line per row on screen, with the cursor as the caret.

Arakun also announces when a session finishes its turn, needs you or fails, for example "Claude Code in arakun/bold-valley needs you". It announces the same events as its desktop notifications.

## Zoom

<kbd>Ctrl+=</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl+-</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl+0</kbd> zoom the whole interface. Settings › Appearance › Zoom does it too.

<Note>
Colors and shape are kept in `palettes.json`, the sidebar layout in `sidebar.json`, next to the state.
</Note>
